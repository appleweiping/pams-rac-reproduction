\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Status boundary.}
This section is a provisional method contract for synchronizing the paper with
the collaborator's frozen implementation. It defines the intended data flow
and the boundary between upstream perception and counting. Components marked
\textbf{SYNC-REQUIRED} are not asserted as implemented or validated. If the
frozen code differs from this contract, the equations and prose must change to
match the code. The narrow supervision statement used throughout this draft is
``without person-wise count or period supervision in the intended counting
objective''; it does not describe the supervision used by the upstream
detector, pose estimator, tracker, or any pretrained representation.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Overview}
\label{sec:problem}

Given a video, an upstream detector, pose estimator, and multi-object tracker
produce \(N\) identity-indexed pose tracks. Track \(i\) consists of joint
coordinates and a validity mask
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \bX_i&=[\mathbf{x}_{i1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{iT_i}]
      \in\mathbb{R}^{T_i\times J\times C}\\
    \bm_i&=[m_{i1},\ldots,m_{iT_i}]\in\{0,1\}^{T_i}
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:track_input}
\end{equation}
Here \(T_i\) is the temporal extent, \(J\) the joint count, \(C\) the coordinate
dimension, and \(m_{it}=1\) denotes a usable pose at time \(t\). Missing
observations keep their original time indices and are masked rather than
deleted. An upstream frontend may additionally expose joint-level visibility
or confidence, but its deterministic reduction to \(m_{it}\) must be recorded
in the synchronized preprocessing contract. The primary prediction is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bc}=[\hat c_1,\ldots,\hat c_N]^{\top}\in\mathbb{N}_0^N
  \label{eq:count_vector}
\end{equation}
The optional total \(\sum_i\hat c_i\) is a diagnostic rather than the task
definition. Under oracle tracks, index \(i\) denotes a ground-truth person and
Equation~\ref{eq:count_vector} is person-wise. Under predicted tracks it denotes
a tracker output, so the raw vector is track-wise: fragmentation may create
several entries for one person and a merge may combine people. Person-wise
predicted-track metrics are defined only after the frozen evaluator's matching
step. The counter cannot undo an identity switch already embedded in its input;
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} therefore separates oracle and predicted tracks.

The design in Figure~\ref{fig:framework} uses a shared pose encoder and a
shared bank of fast, medium, and slow experts. ``Person-specific'' means that
observations, masks, temporal buffers, routing variables, and outputs are
indexed by identity; it does not mean that a separate parameter set is
instantiated for every person or that an identity embedding conditions the
network. Computation is track-indexed and otherwise independent across people.
Each track is processed in overlapping windows,
whose continuous responses are fused before any discrete counting decision.

\subsection{Identity-Conditioned Local Encoding}
\label{sec:encoding}

A single track can accelerate, decelerate, pause, or contain observation gaps,
so we do not assign one speed to the whole person. Let \(L\) and \(S\) denote
window length and stride, and let \(s_{i\ell}\) be the start of window
\(\ell\). Its local input at \(u\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_{i\ell u}=\mathbf{x}_{i,s_{i\ell}+u-1},\qquad
  m_{i\ell u}=m_{i,s_{i\ell}+u-1}
  \label{eq:window_indexing}
\end{equation}
Out-of-range positions are padded with zero mask values. A shared encoder
\(E_\theta\) maps each masked window to embeddings
\begin{equation}
  \bZ_{i\ell}=E_\theta(\bX_{i\ell},\bm_{i\ell})
  =[\mathbf{z}_{i\ell1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{i\ell L}]
  \in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}
  \label{eq:shared_encoder}
\end{equation}
Invalid positions are excluded from aggregation, period evidence, and counting
responses. Independent application of the same encoder places people in one
representation space without pooling their temporal state; \(d\) denotes the
embedding dimension.

\subsection{Local Period Evidence and Soft Routing}
\label{sec:routing}

We derive routing evidence within each window. With a fixed numerical
stabilizer \(\epsilon>0\), the valid-frame mean is
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf z}_{i\ell}=
  \frac{\sum_{u=1}^{L}m_{i\ell u}\mathbf z_{i\ell u}}
  {\sum_{u=1}^{L}m_{i\ell u}+\epsilon}
  \label{eq:valid_mean}
\end{equation}
On the uniform lag grid \(\delta\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\), let
\(n_{i\ell}(\delta)=\sum_{u=1}^{L-\delta}m_{i\ell u}m_{i\ell,u+\delta}\).
Write \(\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{i\ell u}=\mathbf z_{i\ell u}
-\bar{\mathbf z}_{i\ell}\). We define a mask-normalized, non-circular
autocorrelation and deterministically zero-fill unsupported grid locations
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    Q_{i\ell}(\delta)
      &=\sum_{u=1}^{L-\delta}m_{i\ell u}m_{i\ell,u+\delta}
        \langle\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{i\ell u},
        \widetilde{\mathbf z}_{i\ell,u+\delta}\rangle\\
    A_{i\ell}(\delta)
      &=\frac{\mathbf{1}[n_{i\ell}(\delta)\geq n_{\min}]
        Q_{i\ell}(\delta)}{n_{i\ell}(\delta)+\epsilon}
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:masked_acf}
\end{equation}
The support mask is retained separately so a zero-filled lag is never treated
as observed evidence. For a deterministic taper \(h\), let \(\mathcal F\) be
the nonzero frequency bins in
cycles per frame whose reciprocal periods lie inside the declared range. We
use the magnitude of the tapered autocorrelation transform as a routing score,
not as a calibrated physical power spectral density
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    P_{i\ell}(f)&=\left|\operatorname{FFT}(h\odot A_{i\ell})_f\right|\\
    f^*_{i\ell}&=\argmaxop_{f\in\mathcal F}P_{i\ell}(f),\qquad
    \hat p_{i\ell}=1/f^*_{i\ell}
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:local_period}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{i\ell}=
  \frac{P_{i\ell}(f^*_{i\ell})}
  {\sum_{f\in\mathcal F}P_{i\ell}(f)+\epsilon}
  \label{eq:period_confidence}
\end{equation}
Here \(\hat p_{i\ell}\) is measured in frames and is converted to seconds only
with the manifest-bound frame rate. The contract stops the direct gradient
from this evidence branch into the encoder; encoder updates can still change
routing evidence on later optimization steps. A router maps the descriptor
\(\bq_{i\ell}=[\log\hat p_{i\ell},\gamma_{i\ell}]^\top\) to a probability
simplex over shared experts \(\cK=\{\mathrm f,\mathrm m,\mathrm s\}\). Let
\(\boldsymbol{\pi}_0\) be a fixed fallback distribution for a supported but
low-confidence window
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \widetilde{\bg}_{i\ell}
      &=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(R_\psi(\bq_{i\ell})\right)\\
    \bg_{i\ell}
      &=\gamma_{i\ell}\widetilde{\bg}_{i\ell}
        +(1-\gamma_{i\ell})\boldsymbol{\pi}_0\\
    \sum_{k\in\cK}g_{i\ell k}&=1
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:soft_router}
\end{equation}
An eligibility flag \(v_{i\ell}\in\{0,1\}\) is one only when the window meets
the frozen minimum valid-pair, minimum-cycle, activity, and spectral-prominence
rules. If \(v_{i\ell}=0\), the implementation assigns a tensor-shape-only
reference period, sets \(\gamma_{i\ell}=0\), and forces the window response to
zero; it does not route a pause or unsupported window through a counting
expert. The exact activity statistic, thresholds, harmonic-selection rule, and
whether a learned null expert replaces this deterministic gate remain
synchronization-required.
Because \(\bg_{i\ell}\) is local, it may change between adjacent windows of
one identity. Soft assignment also lets an ambiguous transition combine
neighboring experts rather than commit to a hard tempo bin. The minimum
support, confidence calibration, and whether \(\boldsymbol{\pi}_0\) is uniform
or period-conditioned are synchronization-required. Expert-use histograms,
entropy, and specialization curves must diagnose routing collapse.

\subsection{Expert Fusion and Boundary Handling}
\label{sec:fusion}

Each expert \(F_{\phi_k}\) predicts a nonnegative response over one masked
window. Parameters \(\phi_k\) are shared across identities, while each
response remains identity-indexed
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf r^{(k)}_{i\ell}
      &=F_{\phi_k}(\bZ_{i\ell},\bm_{i\ell},\hat p_{i\ell})
        \in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{L}\\
    r_{i\ell u}
      &=v_{i\ell}m_{i\ell u}
        \sum_{k\in\cK}g_{i\ell k}r^{(k)}_{i\ell u}
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:expert_fusion}
\end{equation}
Summing integer window counts creates a direct arithmetic double-counting path
for cycles that straddle an overlap. We instead reconstruct a single response
for each identity. With a taper \(a_u\geq a_{\min}>0\) on every covered frame and
\(\Lambda_i(t)=\{(\ell,u):t=s_{i\ell}+u-1\}\), normalized overlap-add gives
\begin{equation}
  R_{it}=m_{it}
  \frac{\sum_{(\ell,u)\in\Lambda_i(t)}a_u r_{i\ell u}}
  {\sum_{(\ell,u)\in\Lambda_i(t)}a_u m_{i\ell u}+\epsilon}
  \label{eq:normalized_ola}
\end{equation}
The ratio replaces the direct coverage-count scaling of an unnormalized sum
with an \(\epsilon\)-stabilized valid-taper weighted average and excludes
padding from the denominator. Its amplitude can still vary with the responses
being averaged and retains a small support-dependent bias when \(\epsilon>0\),
which the evaluation contract audits explicitly. Only then does a track-level
decoder \(\cD\) extract repetition events
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\cP}_i=
  \cD(\mathbf R_i,\bm_i,\{\hat p_{i\ell},\gamma_{i\ell}\}_{\ell}),\qquad
  \hat c_i=|\widehat{\cP}_i|
  \label{eq:track_decoding}
\end{equation}
Thus overlapping windows never contribute separately rounded counts. Fusion
does not by itself guarantee a single peak when window responses are
phase-misaligned; boundary-straddling tests must measure whether the fused
decoder reduces such errors. The frozen implementation must determine the
taper, coverage rule, peak threshold, period aggregation, and minimum-distance
rule. Constant motion, pauses, contiguous gaps, alternating masks, harmonic
ambiguity, and boundary-straddling cycles are mandatory unit-test cases. The
mask-normalized estimator in Equation~\ref{eq:masked_acf} is not pairwise
variance-normalized, so corruption tests must also quantify mask-induced
spectral artifacts.

For Period-mAP evaluation, the event set must expose more than its cardinality.
The synchronized adapter must export each proposal as
\begin{equation}
  \hat e_{ij}=(i,\hat\tau^{\mathrm{s}}_{ij},
  \hat\tau^{\mathrm{e}}_{ij},\hat s_{ij},\widehat{\mathcal B}_{ij})
  \label{eq:event_schema}
\end{equation}
where the tuple contains its track identity, temporal bounds, confidence, and
the spatial/track support required by the frozen official evaluator. The count
remains \(\hat c_i=|\widehat{\cP}_i|\). Exact interval construction, score
calibration, and evaluator matching remain blocked until that adapter is
bound to code and tests.

\subsection{Training and Synchronization Boundary}
\label{sec:training}

The intended representation objective builds on PAMS-TCC
~\cite{gao2026pams}, which uses temporally adjacent and cyclically
corresponding positives across temporal scales, together with negatives from
other temporal locations, other videos, and different clusters. At the
method-contract level, training is represented by
\begin{equation}
  \cL=\cL_{\mathrm{PAMS\text{-}TCC}}
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{route}}\cL_{\mathrm{route}}
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{track}}\cL_{\mathrm{track}}
  \label{eq:full_objective}
\end{equation}
The reserved routing term should transform assignments equivariantly under
declared tempo changes rather than force invariance to them. By default, the
reserved track term may connect observations only across gaps that retain one
supplied track identity. It does not join fragments carrying different tracker
IDs; any such re-association would be a separate module with its own signal,
supervision disclosure, and evaluation. These are responsibilities, not
recovered loss definitions.

\noindent\textbf{SYNC-REQUIRED.}
Equation~\ref{eq:full_objective} is not yet a complete trainable algorithm: it
does not specify what trains expert responses or the decoder, how experts
specialize, how router collapse is prevented, or how scores are calibrated.
Synchronization must recover the exact architectures, response targets or
self-supervised signals, pseudo-target provenance, formulas, sample
construction, weights, gradient paths, frontend, track normalization, period
range, window schedule, fallback route, and decoding settings. Every use of
count, period, or identity annotations in training, tuning, calibration, and
checkpoint selection must be disclosed. No contribution or result claim
depending on these items is admissible before source anchors, tests, and
ablation evidence exist.
